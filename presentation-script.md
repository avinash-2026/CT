# Presentation Script — "Contextual Thinking: Context Is King"

A slide-by-slide speaking script for presenting `index.html`.

## How to run the deck

- Open `index.html` in a browser. It launches in **deck mode** (one full-screen slide at a time).
- **Advance:** press `→` / `↓` / `Space` / `PageDown`, or click the slide background or the `›` button.
- **Go back:** press `←` / `↑` / `PageUp`, or click the `‹` button.
- **Jump:** `Home` = first slide, `End` = last slide; the side-dots (desktop) jump to any section.
- **Reveals:** several slides reveal their content in steps. Each `→` first uncovers the next block on the current slide, and only advances to the next slide once everything is shown. The **[REVEAL]** cues below mark those clicks.
- Total: **10 slides**. The counter (bottom-right) shows your position.

Timing target: ~5 minutes total. Suggested per-slide times are in each header.

## Slide 1 — Hero: "Contextual Thinking" (~30s)

> "Welcome. Today's idea is simple to say and easy to forget: **the situation decides the meaning — not just the words.**
>
> [REVEAL] Look at the live panel on the right. Same sentence, but watch the context change… and the meaning changes with it. 'OPEN' on a shop door means *come in and buy*. 'OPEN' from a teacher means *open your book*. 'OPEN' from a doctor means *open your mouth*.
>
> The words never moved. The context did all the work. Over the next few minutes we'll unpack why that matters — in conversations, in decisions, and even when you talk to an AI."

*Speaker note: the preview cycles on its own every few seconds; let it play while you talk. Click to move on.*

---

## Slide 2 — What is context? (~30s)

> "So first — what *is* context? Context is the situation. The frame. The background around the information.
>
> [REVEAL] The same words can mean completely different things depending on **how** they're said, **who** said them, **where**, and **when**.
>
> [REVEAL] Two mental pictures. First — context is like a pair of **spectacles**. Without your glasses the letters are blurry; put them on and everything sharpens. Meaning works the same way.
>
> [REVEAL] Second — context is like the **background in a movie**. A character crying with no scene behind them tells you nothing. You need to know who they're with and what just happened. That background is context."

---

## Slide 3 — What is contextual thinking? (~30s)

> "If context is the frame, then **contextual thinking** is the skill of using it. It means looking beyond the information itself to the situation surrounding it — and pulling out the true meaning.
>
> [REVEAL] Here's the formula. Take the **information**: someone says 'I'm fine.' Add the **context** — your partner, at home, right after an argument. And the **output**, the real meaning, is… they are *not* fine. You are in trouble.
>
> Information plus context equals meaning. Miss the middle box and you get the wrong answer."

---

## Slide 4 — The five components (~45s)

> "Contextual thinking isn't one skill — it's five working together. Let me walk the list; watch the constellation light up as I go.
>
> [REVEAL] **One — Notice the situation.** Who's speaking, where, when, and how. Situational awareness.
>
> [REVEAL] **Two — See their point of view.** What does the other person want, feel, or leave unsaid? Perspective-taking.
>
> **Three — Spot familiar patterns.** Use what you've seen before to read the moment faster.
>
> **Four — Change when needed.** Update your read when new information arrives; don't cling to your first guess.
>
> **Five — Combine all the clues.** Words, body language, culture, history, situation — one clue alone is never enough.
>
> Awareness, perspective, patterns, flexibility, integration. Together they form contextual thinking — the 'CT' in the middle."

*Speaker note: hovering a list row lights its matching node — feel free to hover as you name each one.*

---

## Slide 5 — Traditional vs contextual thinking (~40s)

> "Most of us default to **traditional** thinking — fast, word-first, rule-based. Contextual thinking is the upgrade: same information, but you read the situation first.
>
> [REVEAL] Compare them across four dimensions.
>
> [REVEAL] **Focus:** traditional looks at words in isolation; contextual looks at the situation around them. **Speed:** traditional makes a snap judgment; contextual pauses to ask who, where, when, why. **Rules:** one-size-fits-all versus adapts to the case. **Outcome:** technically correct but wrong in practice — versus practically appropriate.
>
> [REVEAL] One example. 'Do whatever you want.' The traditional read: 'Great, full freedom — pineapple pizza it is.' The contextual read: she said it with arms crossed after I forgot our anniversary. There *is* a right answer — and 'whatever I want' is not it.
>
> [REVEAL] Same message, two mindsets. Context is the difference."

---

## Slide 6 — Why we need contextual thinking (~40s)

> "This isn't a nice-to-have. You use it every day, in five ways.
>
> [REVEAL] **One — better communication.** 'Nice job' can be real praise or pure sarcasm; context tells you which.
>
> **Two — better decisions.** The right answer on paper can still be wrong in real life. Context matches your choice to the actual situation.
>
> **Three — understanding people.** 'Reading the room' *is* reading the context — it keeps your relationships intact.
>
> **Four — solving real problems.** Real problems don't have one universal answer; you have to look at what's actually in front of you.
>
> **Five — fairer judgments.** Skip context and you jump to snap verdicts about people. Context lets you judge fairly."

---

## Slide 7 — Why do we get confused? (~35s)

> "So if context is this powerful, why do we still get it wrong? Because our brains are wired for speed — we jump to a meaning before checking the situation.
>
> [REVEAL] **One — fast judgement.** You blow past a 'WET FLOOR' sign because you're moving quickly… and you slip.
>
> [REVEAL] **Two — missing context with AI.** You tell a chatbot 'Fix this' but never paste what 'this' is. You get a generic, useless answer.
>
> [REVEAL] **Three — same word, wrong meaning.** You read 'river bank' but picture a money bank. The surrounding words were telling you all along.
>
> [REVEAL] Concluding without context is like trying to solve a puzzle after seeing only three pieces."

---

## Slide 8 — A small challenge for you (~40s, interactive)

> "Let's test it. Three quick questions — each shows how the situation changes what words mean.
>
> **Q1:** A shop has a big OPEN sign on the door. What does it mean? *(Take a guess… right — the shop is open for customers.)*
>
> **Q2:** 'We sat on the river bank' versus 'I went to the bank for money' — same word? *(No — the surrounding words change the meaning.)*
>
> **Q3:** You ask an AI 'Is this okay?' but never say what 'this' is. What should you do? *(Tell the AI what 'this' is and what you need — it only knows what you type.)*
>
> Notice the pattern: in every case, the *situation* — not the word — held the answer."

*Speaker note: each question card appears on its own `→` press — reveal one, answer it live (click an option to show the color-coded feedback and explanation), then reveal the next.*

---

## Slide 9 — Where to apply contextual thinking (~40s)

> "This skill shows up far beyond texts and the dinner table. Many professions live or die by it.
>
> [REVEAL] **Communication** — reading tone, sarcasm, urgency, and fitting your words to the people and moment.
>
> **Leadership** — choices that fit *your* team, market, and culture, not a copy-pasted playbook.
>
> **Education** — helping students apply ideas in new situations, not just memorize facts.
>
> **Technology / AI** — models only know what you share; full picture in, useful answer out.
>
> **Healthcare** — one symptom can mean many things; doctors read your history and situation, not just the symptom.
>
> **Law & ethics** — applying rules blindly can be unfair; good judges weigh each case on its own.
>
> [REVEAL] In chats, courtrooms, and code — good decisions start with understanding the situation."

---

## Slide 10 — Final takeaway: "Context is king." (~30s)

> "Let me leave you with the whole idea in three words: **context is king.** The situation matters more than the words. The silence more than the sentence. The intent more than the reply.
>
> [REVEAL] Without context — confusion: you read 'STOP' the wrong way and keep going into danger. With context — clarity: a red octagonal STOP sign at an intersection, and you stop safely. With practice — wisdom: checking context becomes automatic, and that means better relationships, better work, a better life.
>
> [REVEAL] So here's your one action. Next time you read a word, a sign, or a sentence, **pause for two seconds** — who? where? when? why? — *then* decide.
>
> Thank you."
